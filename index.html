Executive summary

Remote developers earn more, and graduate degrees are associated with higher pay

An analysis of 20,407 respondents to the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, prepared for a technology company reviewing its remote-work and hiring policies.

Strength of evidence

Two-sample t-test for remote vs. hybrid; every method reaches the same conclusion

Key statistical conclusions

  • Remote workers earn significantly more than hybrid workers: t = −12.9302, p = 4.94 × 10−38 (α = 0.05).
  • The 95% confidence interval for the hybrid − remote gap is −$17,168.87 to −$12,648.73 (formula) and −$17,167.77 to −$12,597.37 (bootstrap); neither includes zero.
  • Medians differ too: Mood's median test p = 4.20 × 10−26.
  • Mean salary differs across education levels in North America: one-way ANOVA F = 25.23, p = 1.28 × 10−11; bootstrap ANOVA p = 0.0001.
  • No clear difference was observed between Master's and Professional-degree holders (about $1.5k apart in bootstrap means); pairwise differences were not formally tested.

What this means for your company

The market pays remote developers noticeably more than hybrid developers, so remote and hybrid roles should be benchmarked separately.

A graduate degree is associated with higher pay in the US and Canada. No clear difference was observed between Master's and Professional degrees, although pairwise differences were not formally tested.

These are associations in survey data, not causal effects: remote roles may differ in seniority, location or job type.

p-value

The p-value is the probability of observing a difference in average salary at least as large as the one in our data if remote and hybrid developers truly earned the same on average. At 4.94 × 10−38, such a gap would be extremely unlikely by chance, so we reject the hypothesis of equal means at the 5% level.

The manual calculation and scipy's built-in test give identical results, so there is no numerical discrepancy. The groups are independent; salaries are right-skewed, but with more than 6,700 people per group the averages are approximately normal (Central Limit Theorem). The ratio of the two variances is about 1.41.

3.d

ANOVA on the bootstrapped data

Each group was shifted to the grand mean (so the null hypothesis holds), resampled 10,000 times and the F-statistic recomputed each time. No bootstrap F reached the observed F = 25.23 (largest: 10.46), so the bootstrap p-value is (0 + 1)/(10,000 + 1).

Bootstrap null distribution of the F-statistic

Grey bars: F values from 10,000 resamples in which the groups truly have equal means. Red line: the observed F.

Business implications

What to consider for your policies

The findings describe the market; they do not show that changing a policy would change pay by these amounts.

01Remote-work policy

Remote roles are paid about $14.9k more on average in this survey, so remote and hybrid pay should be benchmarked separately.

02Compensation structure

Benchmark remote and hybrid pay separately, and check the gap within your own job levels and locations before acting, since seniority and location may drive part of it.

03Educational requirements

A graduate degree is associated with higher pay in North America. No clear difference was observed between Master's and Professional degrees; pairwise differences were not formally tested.

04Data quality

The survey contains impossible ages, scores and salaries. Internal pay surveys should validate currency and pay period at collection.

About the data

Methods, privacy and limitations

Methods

  • Source: cleaned 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey (20,407 respondents with a salary).
  • Outliers: salaries outside the 1st–99th percentile ($655.06 to $440,856) removed in Questions 2 and 3.
  • Bootstrap: 10,000 resamples per group, random seed 42; significance level 0.05.

Privacy

  • Only aggregated results are embedded: summary statistics, test results and binned histogram counts.
  • Figure 3 uses binned counts instead of individual points; only the five impossible-age entries are marked.

Not shown, because not computed in the analysis

  • Post-hoc pairwise tests between education groups (e.g. Tukey HSD) and pairwise bootstrap confidence intervals.
  • Formal variance-equality tests. Causal effects of job mode or education on pay.
